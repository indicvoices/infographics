Descent speed vs cabin impact: six flights compared

Descent speed (average, altitude lost ÷ time)

  • Extreme 100 m/s or more
  • High 50 to 99 m/s
  • Low under 50 m/s

Impact on people and the cabin interior (not airframe damage)

  • Negligible No dive-related injuries or cabin damage reported
  • Moderate Up to about 50 injured, 2 or fewer serious
  • Severe A death, or 10 or more serious injuries

Flydubai FZ107330 Sep 2026 · Boeing 737 MAX 8
~148 m/sExtreme

About 14,125 ft (4,305 m) in 29 s, part of a 17,000+ ft loss in under 2 minutes.[1][2][3][4][5]

Using the rounded 14,000 ft in 30 s gives about 142 m/s.[2]

Peak rate shown: about 157 m/s (30,976 ft/min).[43] Another analysis estimated over 165 m/s (32,600 ft/min), with signals partly garbled.[44]

Public tracking data with gaps during the dive; flight recorder data is needed to confirm.[4]

 Early passenger reports say they did not deploy. Not officially confirmed.[6] Negligible No reports found of overhead bins opening or damage to the cabin interior. Passengers described screaming and blood from the fight.[7][1b] Part of the rudder broke off, and an expert quoted in the reporting said the plane needs at least a complete inspection.[45][46][47] The g-load at pull-out is estimated at about 3 g, above the 737 MAX's 2.5 g operating limit. These are estimates; no recorder data has been released.[45][46][4] Negligible from the dive The captain's deep cuts to the head and hands came from the attack, not the dive; he was serious, later stable.[8][9] First officer also hospitalised.[10] One passenger hospitalised (cause not stated); a few minor injuries; most unharmed.[8] None[11] Landed safely at Tabuk, flown by off-duty pilots travelling as passengers.[8] Passengers continued on a replacement aircraft.[12][14] UAE Attorney-General says the co-pilot attempted a terrorist act.[13]
Singapore Airlines SQ32121 May 2024 · Boeing 777-300ER
~12 m/sLow

178 ft (54.3 m) in 4.6 s, from 37,362 ft to 37,184 ft.[15][16]

A net drop from the peak of an uncommanded climb, attributed most likely to an updraft.[15]

Vertical acceleration swung from negative 1.5 g to positive 1.5 g within 4 seconds.[17]

 Seen hanging from the ceiling in post-landing photos.[17][18] Severe Occupants not belted in became airborne. Gashes in overhead panels, luggage strewn about; passengers hit the overhead lockers.[17][18][19] Severe More than 100 injured.[21] Bangkok hospital: 22 spinal, 6 skull or brain, 13 bone, muscle or other; 20 in intensive care three days later.[20] 173-year-old passenger, suspected heart attack.[17] First death linked to turbulence in civil aviation in 25 years.[22] Diverted and landed safely in Bangkok.[17] Final report: not classic clear-air turbulence; 103 weather-radar problem reports between May 2023 and June 2025.[23]

Read this before comparing

  • Descent speed is average vertical speed: altitude lost divided by the time the sources give. I calculated it; none of the sources state it. China Eastern 5735 and China Airlines 006 are minimums because the sources give only an upper limit on the time. LATAM 800 has no exact duration, so it has no speed.
  • The speed labels and the green, amber and red cabin bands are my own classification, using the thresholds in the legends. They are a reading aid, not an official severity scale.
  • Flydubai figures come from public flight-tracking data, officials, the airline and passenger accounts. The investigation is ongoing and no official findings have been published. The "negligible" rating reflects what has been reported so far.
  • The Flydubai green rating covers people and the cabin interior only. Reports describe part of the rudder breaking off, and the wording varies between sources. The extent of the damage has not been verified by investigators, and the ~3 g figure is an estimate reported by the Wall Street Journal. The rating should not be read as saying the airframe damage was minor.
  • Injury counts differ between sources for Qantas 72 and LATAM 800. The table shows what each source says and flags the difference rather than choosing one.
  • The China Eastern cause is not confirmed by Chinese authorities, and the final report has not been released.
  • These six are documented examples, not a ranking of the largest or fastest altitude losses in commercial aviation.
  • Where a primary source was not available I used reference pages such as Wikipedia and news reports. Primary sources are marked in the list below.
