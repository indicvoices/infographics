West Bengal's new BJP government has announced that ISKCON will take over mid-day meal cooking in Kolkata's government schools — egg and meat off the menu for children whose own families eat fish and eggs.

For context on how other governments have treated this organization: Singapore has banned ISKCON outright since the 1970s. It has never been allowed to register under the Societies Act and is officially classified as an "unregistered and undesirable society" — barred from public worship, preaching, and formal membership activity in the country to this day. Hare Krishna devotees in Singapore only operate by registering under unrelated names (e.g. Sri Krishna Mandir) and explicitly avoiding any ISKCON affiliation.

Sources: Wikipedia — ISKCON · Wikipedia — Freedom of Religion in Singapore · Channel NewsAsia, via NUS Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy

IV · Child sexual abuse
Supreme Court of India · 2025

A petition before the Supreme Court alleged systemic sexual, physical, and psychological abuse of more than 200 children at the hands of senior ISKCON leaders, tied to ISKCON Bangalore's schools. The Court declined to take up the matter directly — instead directing the petitioners to approach the National Commission for Protection of Child Rights, with related representations also to be made to the state child rights commissions in Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal.

Source (LawBeat):
lawbeat.in — Petition Alleges Sexual Abuse in ISKCON Schools
Texas class action · 2000–2005

The darkest chapter. In October 2001, 91 former students filed a $400 million lawsuit in a Texas court against more than a dozen ISKCON temples and organizations, alleging systemic sexual and physical abuse at ISKCON's "gurukula" boarding schools in the US and India through the 1970s–80s.

ISKCON's response: Chapter 11 bankruptcy filings by the named temples. In 2005, a federal bankruptcy court ordered a $9.5 million settlement, eventually distributed to roughly 450 claimants — a wider pool than the original 91 plaintiffs — with individual payouts ranging from $2,500 to $50,000 depending on the severity of abuse.

Harvard Pluralism Project Archive, citing Hindustan Times

CBS News, 2002
Bhakti Vidya Purna Swami · ISKCON Child Protection Office decision, October 2022

ISKCON's own Child Protection Office found sitting guru Bhakti Vidya Purna Swami (BVPS) responsible for sexually abusing a minor and engaging in "gross sexual behavior" with a young woman — this after he'd already admitted in 2000 to "imposing excessive physical punishments" on boys at the school he ran, and was found to have known about other abuse and ignored it.

"Whilst BVPS was sitting on the vyasasana in the temple room and teaching the children at the schools, he would preach about Vedic principles. However, in his private life, he was sexually molesting a minor and engaging in gross sexual behavior with a young woman, which he has admitted to."

He was hit with lifetime restrictions — banned from Mayapur, from leadership, and from any role involving children — but retains his guru title with his existing disciples.

Source (ISKCON News, the organization's own outlet):
iskconnews.org — Ban on Bhakti Vidya Purna Swami
Lokanath Swami · 1990–present

A girl named Satya has stated that senior, currently active guru Lokanath Swami molested her in 1990, when she was 11. In a 2011 letter, Lokanath Swami himself wrote: "During my visit, there were some very regrettable incidents that took place between me and their eleven and a half year old daughter Satya, in which I sexually abused her." A 1993 GBC committee investigated the matter; according to reporting by Be Scofield, ISKCON leadership did not refer the case to its Child Protection Office for decades.

In 2021, leaked internal documents triggered renewed public attention — and a suspension that was reversed five days later. In 2022, ISKCON's top body (the GBC) voted to keep him as guru. One member, Praghosa Dasa, resigned after 25 years in protest, citing the decision. ISKCON North America and ISKCON Europe subsequently restricted or banned him within their own territories.

"If there were cases with high-profile people... it got into dark, ugly, twisted, political manipulations. As years went on, it got less about helping the children themselves and more about dealing with politics." — David Wolf, former Director, ISKCON Child Protection Office

ISKCON India leadership · 2021

When ISKCON's global body moved in May 2021 to refer Lokanath Swami's case to its own Child Protection Office, ISKCON's India leadership, according to reporting by Be Scofield, opposed that referral — pressuring the GBC to keep the case out of CPO review and keep him installed as guru. Lokanath remains one of the most prominent ISKCON figures in India.

Dhanurdhara Swami (Denis Winiker) · Vrindavan Boys Gurukula, 1979–1995

Dhanurdhara Swami (Denis Winiker) ran ISKCON's Vrindavan Boys Gurukula in two stretches between 1979 and 1995. ISKCON's own Governing Body Commission later admitted in an official statement that "his violence was extreme and extensive against the boys." The CPO banned him from all leadership; he simply left ISKCON in 2006 and kept initiating disciples independently, including in Vrindavan itself.

VI · Criminal cases, violence & financial-fraud allegations involving ISKCON-affiliated individuals
Kirtanananda Swami (Keith Ham) · Federal indictment, 1990

The US federal government indicted ISKCON guru Kirtanananda Swami on five counts of racketeering, six counts of mail fraud, and conspiracy to murder two of his opponents within ISKCON — Steven Bryant and Charles St. Denis. Prosecutors alleged he had illegally amassed over $10.5 million in 4 years, and that he ordered the killings because the victims were threatening to expose his child sexual abuse. He was separately found guilty of child sexual abuse by ISKCON's own Child Protection Office.

Source (Wikipedia, citing federal indictment + court records):
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirtanananda_Swami
Murder of Steven Bryant (Sulochan Das) · May 1986

One of Kirtanananda's victims, Steven Bryant, had spent months warning police his life was in danger after accusing the New Vrindavan temple of child abuse, drug dealing, and "systematic violence." Police didn't take him seriously. He was shot dead in his van in Los Angeles. A federal grand jury was convened to investigate both the murder and drug trafficking allegations at New Vrindavan.

Sources:
Hinduism Today, Oct. 1986 — "Murder Quickens Wider Crisis in Krishna Sect"
TIME, 1986 — "Troubled Karma for the Krishnas"
Drug trafficking · California temple, 1979

The drug angle isn't new — TIME reported that suspicions about drug activity connected to an ISKCON-affiliated community go back to 1979, when a California temple leader, Alexander Kulik, was convicted of distributing heroin and accused of laundering drug money through a sect-controlled investment company.

TIME — "Troubled Karma for the Krishnas"
"Krishnas: Gurus. Karma. Murder." · Peacock documentary, 2023

A 2023 Peacock documentary put the whole story back in the spotlight, examining the "culture of secrecy and exploitation" Keith Ham built at New Vrindavan — including the child abuse and the murder conspiracy. ISKCON's own official response confirmed it "gave its full cooperation to law enforcement officials who prosecuted and eventually jailed Ham," and that New Vrindavan itself was expelled from ISKCON in 1988 as a result.

VII · ISKCON & the mid-day meal scheme
Karnataka Assembly · 2013

Senior Congress leader DK Shivakumar accused ISKCON's mid-day meal arm, Akshaya Patra, of "massive financial fraud," called it a "family business," and demanded a CBI probe into its government-funded operations.

Source (DNA India):
dnaindia.com — Akshaya Patra fraud allegations
Akshaya Patra Foundation · 2014–2020

The 2013 fraud allegations were not the subject of any public probe in the years that followed. Separately, between 2014 and 2015, independent trustees noticed the cost per meal had risen sharply, from around ₹6 to ₹12–13, and in 2016 pushed for an audit committee. Chaired by former Wipro CFO Suresh Senapati, the committee spent a year digging and found 50–60 people who weren't actually working for the foundation — but for ISKCON's affiliated temple trusts — being paid out of Akshaya Patra's account. Whistle-blower complaints followed, alleging the foundation's kitchens and resources were being diverted to ISKCON's own parallel businesses and temple construction.

One independent trustee, Abhay Jain, resigned in February 2020; the remaining three independent trustees — all serving since the foundation's founding in 2000 — resigned in October, saying their push for transparency had gone nowhere. Around this period, ISKCON Bengaluru's own president and senior vice president took over as Chairman and Vice Chairman of the Akshaya Patra board.

Sources:
The Quint — Why Mohandas Pai & Others Resigned From Akshaya Patra Foundation
The Wire — The Allegations Against Akshaya Patra, and Why a Probe Is Needed
CAG report to the Ministry of Human Resource Development · 2015

187 of ISKCON's tested meal samples failed to meet prescribed nutrition standards, with negative feedback from 75% of children and teachers surveyed — and serving grain quantities far below the mandated 100–150g per meal (children were getting roughly 40g).

Karnataka · 2018–19

Akshaya Patra openly defied a state government directive to add onions, garlic, and eggs — citing its own religious dietary rules over government nutrition policy. Karnataka's Food Commission separately found the food "bland and monotonous," with milk served cold or spoiled, in violation of scheme norms.

thenewsminute.com — Food Commission meeting
Parliamentary Committee on Welfare of SCs/STs · 2013

Flagged a separate concern: "unauthorised and illegal collection of donations or contributions by ISKCON and Akshaya Patra from public in India and abroad," tied to a scheme that's supposed to be government-funded.

Source (Scroll.in):
scroll.in — No Onion, No Garlic
Internal audit, Akshaya Patra Foundation · 2020

An internal audit committee itself flagged multiple whistle-blower complaints against its own unit presidents over a two-year period — concerns serious enough that government officials separately had to order the organization to stop displaying its own logo more prominently than the government's official mid-day meal branding.

Source (The Federal, citing internal audit + The Wire):
thefederal.com — Inquiry into Akshaya Patra funds
Chandigarh, Karnataka, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh · Various

Chandigarh's education department tested ISKCON's onion/garlic-free meals and found them unpalatable to students — the mid-day meal contract was simply never awarded there. Multiple states have had open standoffs with Akshaya Patra over its refusal to serve eggs, despite National Institute of Nutrition recommendations of 3 eggs/week for growing children.

West Bengal now joins this list — a state where fish and eggs are dietary staples. Critics argue that Akshaya Patra has, in several states, prioritized its own religious dietary standards over government nutrition recommendations, and the organization has also faced financial-governance and oversight criticism in a number of the jurisdictions where it operates.
